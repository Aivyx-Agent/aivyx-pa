# Teams

For big jobs, one assistant can lead a **team** of specialists — a crew where a
lead delegates steps to members, each holding only the access its role needs.
This is how Aivyx PA tackles work that's too broad or too sensitive for a single
agent.

## How a team works

A team is a **lead** plus up to eight **specialists** — nine members in all,
which is where the built-in team's name, the *Nonagon*, comes from. Each
member has:

- a **role** (what it's responsible for),
- a **character** (how it approaches its work),
- a tightly scoped set of **tools and permissions** — only what its job requires.

The lead receives the goal, breaks it into steps, delegates each step to the
best-fit specialist, checks their work, and assembles the result. Specialists
can't reach beyond their lane — a member that only logs records, for example,
physically cannot send a message or spend money.

## Why the scoping matters

This is least-privilege by design. Even inside an autonomous job, the
consequential actions are confined to the one member authorized for them, and
that member still hits an **approval gate** for anything irreversible. A team
multiplies what your assistant can take on without multiplying what can go wrong.

## The Teams screen

The **Teams** screen shows your active team — the lead and each specialist, their
roles, what they're trusted to do, and the tools they hold. You can also edit the
roster here: adjust roles, characters, and scopes, within the nine-member limit.
Each member can even use its own model.

## Vertical packs

A team can be tailored to a domain. Aivyx PA ships an example — a
back-of-house kitchen crew (a manager leading stocktake, inventory, purchasing,
and food-safety specialists) — that shows how a team plus a set of domain tools
becomes a ready-made expert crew for a specific line of work. These domain
bundles are called *vertical packs*; more can be added without changing the core
assistant.
